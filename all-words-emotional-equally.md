# All words are emotional equally considered the same one time — thread note

*Douglas's doctrine, delivered verbatim Oct 15/16, 2026 in the "Change of Address" side chat. Standing reading rule for the book.*

**The rule as given:** Every word carries equal emotional weight when weighed once, completely.

**What it demands of the writing itself:**
- No throwaway words. If every word weighs the same, there is no filler, no ornament, no word that gets to hide.
- No hierarchy of words. A small word like "and" has to be chosen with the same care as a big word like "eternity."
- Plainspoken by necessity. Showing off breaks the rule — a fancy word used to impress is a word weighed twice.
- Written and read slowly. "Considered the same one time" means weighed once, completely — not skimmed, not rushed past.
- Honesty as structure. If all words are equal, you cannot use ten soft words to smuggle in one hard meaning. Say it plain or don't say it.

**Open question for Douglas:** Is there any word he thinks carries MORE — and if so, does the rule still hold?
